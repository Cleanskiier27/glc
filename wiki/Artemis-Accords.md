# The Artemis Accords

[← Back to Wiki Home](README.md)

## Overview

The **Artemis Accords** are a set of non-binding, bilateral agreements between
the United States and other nations, establishing a shared framework of
principles for cooperation in the civil exploration and use of the Moon,
Mars, comets, and asteroids. They are named after the **Artemis program**,
NASA's initiative to return humans to the Moon.

The Accords were first signed on **October 13, 2020**, by NASA and space
agencies from seven other founding nations, and have since been signed by
dozens of additional countries.

## Founding Signatories (2020)

- United States
- Australia
- Canada
- Italy
- Japan
- Luxembourg
- United Arab Emirates
- United Kingdom

Many more countries have joined in the years since, making the Accords one of
the broadest international frameworks for space cooperation.

## Key Principles

1. **Peaceful purposes** — All activities must be conducted for peaceful
   purposes, in line with the Outer Space Treaty.
2. **Transparency** — Signatories agree to publicly describe their policies
   and plans for space exploration.
3. **Interoperability** — Support for common exploration infrastructure and
   standards to enable international cooperation.
4. **Emergency assistance** — Commitment to render assistance to astronauts
   in distress.
5. **Registration of space objects** — Signatories agree to register space
   objects in accordance with the Registration Convention.
6. **Release of scientific data** — Commitment to publicly share scientific
   data generated by their activities.
7. **Preserving heritage** — Protection of outer space heritage sites, such
   as historic Apollo landing sites.
8. **Space resources** — Recognition that the extraction and use of space
   resources (e.g., lunar ice, regolith) can be conducted in a manner
   consistent with the Outer Space Treaty, without constituting national
   appropriation of celestial bodies.
9. **Deconfliction of activities** — Coordination between signatories to
   avoid harmful interference, including the establishment of "safety zones"
   around operations.
10. **Orbital debris and spacecraft disposal** — Commitment to plan for the
    safe disposal of spacecraft and mitigation of orbital debris.

## Relationship to the Outer Space Treaty

The Artemis Accords build upon and reaffirm commitments made under the 1967
**Outer Space Treaty**, which prohibits national appropriation of celestial
bodies and requires that space be used for peaceful purposes. The Accords add
more specific, operational guidance for how nations can cooperate on
lunar and deep-space missions consistent with that treaty.

## Significance

The Accords are notable for:

- Establishing a common set of "rules of the road" for the growing number of
  nations and private actors participating in lunar exploration.
- Addressing previously ambiguous questions, such as how space resource
  utilization can be reconciled with existing international space law.
- Serving as a diplomatic tool to build coalitions of partner nations around
  NASA's Artemis program and future Moon-to-Mars exploration efforts.

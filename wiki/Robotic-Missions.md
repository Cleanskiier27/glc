# Robotic Missions

[← Back to Wiki Home](README.md)

Uncrewed robotic spacecraft have explored nearly every part of the Solar
System, often reaching destinations far too hazardous or distant for crewed
missions.

## Notable Robotic Missions

| Mission | Agency | Launched | Target/Achievement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Voyager 1 & 2** | NASA | 1977 | First spacecraft to explore the outer planets; Voyager 1 is the most distant human-made object, now in interstellar space |
| **Viking 1 & 2** | NASA | 1975 | First successful Mars landers |
| **Hubble Space Telescope** | NASA/ESA | 1990 | Landmark orbital observatory |
| **Cassini–Huygens** | NASA/ESA/ASI | 1997 | Orbited Saturn; Huygens probe landed on Titan |
| **Mars rovers** (Sojourner, Spirit, Opportunity, Curiosity, Perseverance) | NASA | 1996–present | Surface exploration and sample analysis on Mars |
| **New Horizons** | NASA | 2006 | First flyby of Pluto (2015) |
| **Rosetta/Philae** | ESA | 2004 | First spacecraft to orbit and land on a comet |
| **Hayabusa/Hayabusa2** | JAXA | 2003/2014 | Asteroid sample-return missions |
| **Chang'e program** | CNSA | 2007–present | Lunar orbiters, landers, and sample-return |
| **James Webb Space Telescope** | NASA/ESA/CSA | 2021 | Infrared observatory succeeding Hubble |

## Why Robotic Missions Matter

- They can travel to destinations too dangerous or distant for humans,
  such as the outer planets or deep space.
- They are generally far less expensive and lower-risk than crewed missions.
- They often serve as precursors, gathering data used to plan future crewed
  missions.

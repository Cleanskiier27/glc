# History of Space Exploration

[← Back to Wiki Home](README.md)

## The Early Era

- **1942** — The German V-2 rocket becomes the first human-made object to reach
  space (crossing the 100 km Kármán line), though it was developed as a weapon.
- **1957** — The Soviet Union launches **Sputnik 1**, the first artificial
  satellite, marking the start of the Space Age and the beginning of the
  Cold War-era "Space Race."
- **1958** — The United States creates **NASA** (National Aeronautics and
  Space Administration) in response to Sputnik.
- **1961** — **Yuri Gagarin** becomes the first human in space, orbiting Earth
  aboard *Vostok 1*.

## The Race to the Moon

- **1962** — President John F. Kennedy announces the goal of landing a person
  on the Moon before the end of the decade.
- **1969** — **Apollo 11** lands the first humans, Neil Armstrong and Buzz
  Aldrin, on the Moon on July 20, 1969.
- **1969–1972** — Five more Apollo missions successfully land on the Moon,
  culminating in Apollo 17.

## The Shuttle and Station Era

- **1971** — The Soviet Union launches **Salyut 1**, the first space station.
- **1981** — NASA's **Space Shuttle** program begins, enabling reusable
  crewed spaceflight.
- **1998** — Construction begins on the **International Space Station (ISS)**,
  a multinational collaboration between NASA, Roscosmos, ESA, JAXA, and CSA.
- **2011** — The Space Shuttle program ends after 135 missions.

## The Modern Era

- **2004–present** — Private companies (SpaceX, Blue Origin, Virgin Galactic,
  and others) begin developing launch vehicles and crewed spacecraft,
  ushering in the era of commercial spaceflight.
- **2020** — SpaceX's Crew Dragon becomes the first commercial spacecraft to
  carry astronauts to the ISS.
- **2022** — NASA's **Artemis I** mission launches, beginning a new program
  aimed at returning humans to the Moon and eventually reaching Mars.

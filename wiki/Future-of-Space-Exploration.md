# The Future of Space Exploration

[← Back to Wiki Home](README.md)

## Returning to the Moon

NASA's **Artemis program** aims to land astronauts on the Moon again,
including the first woman and person of color to do so, and to establish a
sustainable lunar presence via the planned **Gateway** space station and
surface infrastructure. Other nations, including China with its planned
crewed lunar missions, are also targeting the Moon this decade.

## Mars and Beyond

- **Human missions to Mars** are a long-term goal for NASA and a stated
  priority for SpaceX, which is developing the **Starship** vehicle
  specifically for interplanetary transport.
- **Robotic sample-return** missions, such as the Mars Sample Return
  campaign, aim to bring Martian soil and rock samples back to Earth for
  detailed laboratory analysis.

## Commercial Spaceflight

Private companies are increasingly taking on roles once reserved for
government agencies:

- **Commercial space stations** are being developed to succeed the ISS,
  which is planned for retirement in the late 2020s/early 2030s.
- **Space tourism** is expanding, with companies offering suborbital and
  orbital flights to private citizens.
- **Reusable launch vehicles** continue to drive down the cost of reaching
  orbit, enabling more frequent and affordable missions.

## Emerging Technologies

- **In-situ resource utilization (ISRU)** — Using local resources (e.g.,
  lunar ice, Martian regolith) to produce fuel, water, and building
  materials, reducing the need to launch everything from Earth.
- **Nuclear propulsion** — Nuclear thermal and nuclear electric propulsion
  are being researched to enable faster transit times to Mars and beyond.
- **Space telescopes** — Successors to the James Webb Space Telescope aim to
  directly image Earth-like exoplanets and search for signs of life.
- **Space debris mitigation** — As orbital traffic increases, technologies
  for tracking and removing debris are becoming increasingly important.

## Open Questions

- Is there evidence of past or present life on Mars, Europa, or Enceladus?
- Can humans achieve sustainable, long-term habitation beyond Earth?
- How will international law govern resource extraction on the Moon and
  asteroids?

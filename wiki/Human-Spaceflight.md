# Human Spaceflight

[← Back to Wiki Home](README.md)

## Milestones

- **1961** — Yuri Gagarin becomes the first human in space (Vostok 1).
- **1963** — Valentina Tereshkova becomes the first woman in space.
- **1965** — Alexei Leonov performs the first spacewalk (EVA).
- **1969** — Apollo 11 lands the first humans on the Moon.
- **1971** — The first space station, Salyut 1, is launched and crewed.
- **1998–present** — The International Space Station provides a continuous
  human presence in low Earth orbit.
- **2020** — SpaceX Crew Dragon becomes the first commercial crewed spacecraft
  to reach orbit and dock with the ISS.

## The International Space Station (ISS)

The ISS is a modular space station in low Earth orbit, roughly 400 km above
the surface. It has been continuously occupied since November 2000, making it
humanity's longest-running crewed outpost in space. It serves as a
microgravity research laboratory for biology, physics, astronomy, and
materials science.

## Challenges of Human Spaceflight

- **Microgravity effects** — Prolonged weightlessness causes muscle atrophy,
  bone density loss, and fluid shifts in the body.
- **Radiation exposure** — Outside Earth's protective magnetosphere,
  astronauts face increased exposure to cosmic and solar radiation.
- **Life support** — Spacecraft must recycle air and water and manage waste
  in a closed environment.
- **Psychological factors** — Isolation, confinement, and distance from Earth
  present significant mental health challenges on long-duration missions.

## Looking Ahead

Programs like **Artemis** aim to return humans to the Moon and establish a
sustainable presence there as a stepping stone toward future crewed missions
to Mars.
